2. Not a Substitute for Professional Judgment

FracSTAT computes geometric statistics (length, orientation, connectivity, intensity), stress-resolution metrics (slip tendency, dilation tendency, fracture susceptibility, Mohr–Coulomb failure state), and uncertainty-quantification outputs from the data and stress assumptions a user supplies. Every output described throughout this documentation — for example the tendency and failure-state metrics explained on the Method Tab page, or the distribution-fitting results on the CDF Tab page — is conditional on the input geometry and stress model actually entered, and is intended as a decision-support aid, not a decision by itself. Interpretation of any result requires the judgment of a qualified structural geologist, geotechnical engineer, or geomechanics specialist familiar with the specific field area, dataset, and intended application.

3. Accuracy of Calculations and Statistics

Wherever practical, this documentation reproduces the exact formulas FracSTAT implements (for example, the Mohr–Coulomb stress-resolution equations, the Mauldon circular-scanline intensity/density estimators, and the maximum-likelihood distribution-fitting procedure used on the CDF tab) so that results can, in principle, be independently verified by hand or with separate software. Numerical results nonetheless depend on floating-point arithmetic, on the specific numerical tolerances and clamping rules documented in each tab's own page (e.g. the segment-intersection tolerances described on the Connectivity Tab page), and on any third-party library FracSTAT depends on (NumPy, SciPy, the powerlaw package, and others). Users performing safety- or economically-significant work are encouraged to cross-check key results against an independent calculation.

4. Dependence on Input Data Quality

Every statistic FracSTAT reports is only as reliable as the digitized trace map, Excel/CSV table, or calibrated photograph supplied to it. Digitization resolution, sampling-area edge-truncation and censoring effects, scale-calibration accuracy, and dip-override data quality (all discussed in their respective tab pages, e.g. the edge-censoring discussion on the Histogram Tab page) directly propagate into every downstream statistic, plot, and export. FracSTAT does not, and cannot, independently verify the geological accuracy of data a user chooses to load.
